# Viewfinder

> What a broadcast camera operator sees: framing guides, safe areas, exposure warnings and running timecode, over one of my own stadium frames.

Interactive version: https://thebenn.me/lab/viewfinder/

This is a frame from one of my camera positions at a Slovenian football broadcast, with the overlays a broadcast viewfinder shows the operator. Turn each one on and off below.

## The overlays

- **Rule of thirds.** Two lines each way. Putting the subject on a crossing point usually beats putting it dead centre.
- **Action safe and title safe.** Older TVs cropped the edges of the picture, so broadcasters keep the action inside the outer box (93% of the frame) and all text inside the inner box (90%). The habit survives because many screens and players still crop.
- **Centre mark.** A small cross so you can find the middle of the frame without thinking.
- **Zebras.** Diagonal stripes over the brightest parts of the picture, warning that they are close to clipping to pure white. On a sunny pitch you watch the white lines and the sky.
- **Focus box.** Follows your pointer. Click the frame to take the shot.

## Timecode

The counter at the top is SMPTE-style timecode: hours, minutes, seconds and frames, counting 25 frames per second. Every camera and recorder in a broadcast truck runs on the same timecode, so the replay operator and the editor can find the exact same frame later.
